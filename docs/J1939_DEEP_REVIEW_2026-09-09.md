# J1939+2134 bounded deep review — 9 September 2026

Authorized by the owner: perform the same depth of follow-up used for J1453,
and make deep review standard for future compelling candidates.

The original InPTA result remains a consumed 567.405-day conditional trigger,
statistic 149.353 versus threshold 26.713. This review is a separate analysis.

## Fixed scope

- Preserve all 18,191 released/prepared TOAs, their 157 observing days, timing
  nuisance design and the original result. The covariance is verified diagonal.
- Compare 16 specified noise choices: white-error scale 1 or 0.5, each with
  white only; achromatic Fourier noise (gamma 2, RMS 1 us; gamma 4, RMS 1/2/4 us);
  dispersion-like gamma-2 noise of RMS 1 us at 400 MHz; frequency^-4 gamma-2
  noise of RMS 1 us at 400 MHz; or their specified joint combination.
  Each process uses 30 frequencies k/T, with spectral weights normalized to
  the quoted process RMS. These are a bounded diagnostic family, not newly
  measured physical noise parameters.
- Evaluate the original eligible frequency grid plus a 400–800-day refinement
  at 0.5-day spacing. Retain the original survey threshold without applying it
  to subsets or the alternative-noise statistic.
- Generate 512 noise-only realizations and 256 injections, seed 2026092401.
  Reselect the noise model separately under null and sinusoid, and search the
  same combined grid for each. Recovery tolerance is 30 days; injection phase
  and amplitude come from the released-noise refined fit.
- Remove every observing day in turn at the refined fixed period. Compare
  time halves and actual receiver bands divided at 1000 MHz: 17,765 low-band
  and 426 high-band TOAs. This addresses the unidentifiable 1400-MHz subset
  in the initial review.
- Fit the fixed candidate period in PPTA full data and the common time window;
  compare InPTA overlap and later data, including coefficient uncertainties.

environment. The diagonal-plus-Fourier likelihood uses exact Woodbury algebra
with timing nuisance parameters marginalized. One small dense comparison checks
the algebra; the original full-data statistic must reproduce. Two direct fits
check the observing-day sufficient-statistic calculation. Qualified scientific
source and original calibration are unchanged.

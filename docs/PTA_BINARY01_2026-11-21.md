## Things to know

- **Twelve of these pulsars had never been searched by this project**:
  J0614−3329, J0900−3144, J1045−4509, J1125−6014, J1446−4701, J1545−4550,
  J1643−1224, J1902−5105 and J2129−5721 (Parkes), and J1751−2857,
  J1804−2717 and J1910+1256 (European). J0900−3144 was searched in both
  releases. The other searches are longer or independent records of
  pulsars searched before in MeerKAT or NANOGrav data.
- **Eleven searches reach 2,000 days.** That is the range where the
  population limit rested on about twenty pulsars.
- **Three are searches in name only**, because a wide inner orbit leaves
  little room: J0614−3329 (2 trial periods), Parkes J0900−3144 (7),
  Parkes J1643−1224 (7). European J1751−2857 has 12.
- **J1446−4701** has a null reduced chi-square of 0.82: the released noise
  model over-describes the scatter, which makes its limit conservative.
  The others are 0.92–1.03.
- **Parkes uncertainties.** Under the owner's rule the Parkes targets use
  the search's own uncertainty for the orbit checks, because the release
  gives none.
- **Calibration diagnostic.** The bounded injection check recovered 87–96
  of 100 at the stated 95% amplitudes (J1643−1224: 87, from three trial
  periods). It is a diagnostic on 96 injections, not a pass or fail.
- Minimum masses are 95% detection sensitivities for circular orbits at
  the worst phase, a 1.6 M☉ inner system and fixed released noise. They
  are first-stage, fixed-noise figures.
- The noise of a pulsar searched in two releases is not independent
  between them; these are not independent trials of the same sky.

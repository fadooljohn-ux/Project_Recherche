# B1257+12 known-system benchmark

The owner authorized the bounded route on 2026-09-08: obtain observations,
calibrate for this dataset, recover the two larger planets, attempt the smaller
planet, and compare with published parameters. Local compatibility repairs and
execution are included. The B1937+21 result and qualification remain preserved.

## Dataset and implementation

Use Susarla's I-LOFAR release, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14865192, associated with
https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09549. Original ZIPs and the target's PAR, TIM and DM
files are retained outside Git in `../Project Recherche Data/b1257-benchmark-20260908/`.
The downloaded ZIPs match the publisher's sizes and MD5 checksums; acquisition
also records SHA-256. The target contains 310 narrowband TOAs in 37 observing
epochs over approximately 735 days. The published scatter is about 30 microseconds.

The supplied TEMPO2 T2 model includes all three orbits, with an unphysical
negative fitted inner period and projected semimajor axis. Preserve the original.
Remove all orbit parameters from the analysis starting model; use PINT's TCB-to-TDB
conversion and the repaired spin-phase evaluation. Register the missing IE613
site from upstream TEMPO2 observatory coordinates. Reuse locally bound DE440
and IERS resources. Setup found that TT(BIPM2019) does not cover the complete
2022–2024 observations; bind the published TT(BIPM2025) clock instead and retain
strict clock-coverage checks. The failed setup log is preserved; no scan ran.
This is a new narrowband benchmark adapter,
not an extension of B1937's qualification claims.

## Bounded analysis, specified before the first scan

- Search 10–400 days on the existing uniform-frequency grid at oversampling 5.
- Project timing nuisance terms and one dispersion term per observing epoch;
  do not count the paired released DM series as independent measurements.
- Use supplied TOA uncertainties, inflated by the larger of 1 and the square
  root of the released reduced chi-square. Measure remaining fit quality and
  report any evidence that this noise assumption is inadequate.
- Recalculate the annual projection sensitivity on this dataset. Exclude cells
  where timing/astrometric projection removes more than 80% of a possible
  sinusoid's weighted power remaining after epoch-DM separation. Store the
  complete map; do not import B1937's mask.
- Calibrate scan maxima with 4,096 Gaussian null vectors using a fixed seed.
  Use the larger of the empirical 99th percentile and a conservative analytical
  threshold `2 log(3 * number_of_frequencies / 0.01)` for up to three searches.
- Recover the strongest signal, fit it, then search the remaining data while
  projecting the fitted signal. Jointly refine the two large signals with
  eccentric Keplerian delays before testing for the small signal. No known
  planet period is supplied as an initial search peak.
- Assess the 25.26-day planet with the same residual scan, then a labelled
  fixed-published-period amplitude estimate whether or not the scan triggers.
- Use fixed-seed injections at published signal amplitudes to measure this
  dataset's recovery capability, especially for the weak inner planet.
- Compare periods and projected timing amplitudes, not true masses or inclinations.
  Two-Keplerian fits do not reproduce the full interacting dynamical solution.
- This is a known-system recovery benchmark with conditional noise assumptions,
  not a new discovery search. Preserve intermediate and final results, including
  non-recovery. Do not tune thresholds to force recovery.

## Result: two planets recovered; inner planet below demonstrated sensitivity

The bounded observed benchmark is complete. The two stronger periodicities were
selected without supplying their published periods to the search. The final
joint fit gives:

| Planet | Published period (days) | Recovered period (days) | Published projected amplitude (us) | Recovered projected amplitude (us) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| c (historical B) | 66.5419 | 66.6218 +/- 0.1002 | 1310.6 | 1311.2 |
| d (historical C) | 98.2114 | 98.0558 +/- 0.1602 | 1413.4 | 1389.1 |
| b (historical A) | 25.262 | Not recovered | 3.0 | Not a detection |

Period uncertainties are local formal errors conditional on the fixed scaled
Gaussian TOA covariance and the independent-Keplerian model. Both recovered
periods agree with the historical values within those errors. Projected timing
amplitudes agree to about 0.05% (c) and 1.7% (d). No true mass or inclination
measurement is claimed, and the small fitted eccentricities are not precision
reproductions of the interacting system.

The complete 360-cell, 10–400-day grid has 16 ineligible cells under the new
projection map. The 4,096-null empirical 99th percentile is 19.07282. The
conservative analytical three-search threshold is larger, **23.1797730122**, and
was retained unchanged throughout. Initial detection statistics were 4,872.07
and 5,488.45. After the final joint fit the largest remaining eligible statistic
was only 2.97975, at 15.365 days. At the published 25.262-day period, the statistic
was 0.04296. Its best-fit 2.25-us amplitude has coefficient uncertainties of about
13–15 us and is **not a detection**.

Final chi-square is 131.119 for 255 degrees of freedom (reduced chi-square
0.514). This indicates conservative errors and/or more structure in the error
model than independent Gaussian noise; it is not a measurement of a new noise
process. The original uncertainty scale was retained, not reduced to increase
sensitivity. The threshold is a conditional benchmark criterion, not a measured
end-to-end false-alarm probability after adaptive orbital fitting.

The published 3-us inner signal was recovered in **0/256** phase-randomized
Gaussian injections, both in the full-grid search and at the exact period under
the same threshold. This dataset cannot demonstrate reliable inner-planet
recovery with this method. Failure to recover b is not evidence against b.

A separate fixed-seed 32-case simulation of all three signals through the actual
sampling recovered both large planets in **31/32** cases. It never read the
observed residual vector. The retained miss selected sampling/interference peaks
near 87 and 62 days and then encountered the local joint-fit boundary. This is
a demonstrated limitation of the bounded sequential search, not a 100% recovery
claim. No additional search over alternative peaks was added to force that case.

## Repairs and verification

The first observed joint fit hit its upper frequency bound: a single-planet fit
had been biased by the second strong signal, and the original one-oversampled-cell
refinement interval could not reach the joint solution. Preserved that result in
`run01`. Refit the two original selected peaks over one independent frequency
bin, with no published-period seed. The final fit in `refinement01` has no active
parameter bounds. Only the third residual scan and conditional sensitivity were
repeated after this repair; the first two scans and threshold were reused.

Three focused numerical checks passed, covering phase wrapping, independent
eccentric Roemer-delay recovery (including a displaced initial frequency), and
separation of epoch-DM information loss from annual astrometric degeneracy.
Static checks passed. All 71 qualified scientific source files remain unchanged.
This adapter is a bounded narrowband benchmark; it does not inherit B1937's
formal qualification or constitute general automatic per-pulsar calibration.

The first calibration setup exposed an adapter error: measuring the annual
mask against raw TOAs counted the cost of separating dispersion at every period,
excluding the entire grid. Before any observed scan, corrected the reference to
the dispersion-projected signal. The timing exclusion still uses 80% loss;
all dispersion-related uncertainty remains in the covariance and threshold
calibration. Removed redundant global DM/solar-wind design columns because
epoch-DM columns replace them. Failed setup artifacts are retained.

Actual filename grouping gives 37 epochs, correcting the initial estimate of
31 from ten channels per epoch. Not all epochs retain all ten channels.
The median individual TOA uncertainty is 24.53 microseconds. The inner planet's
published projected timing amplitude is only 3.0 microseconds, compared with
1,310.6 and 1,413.4 microseconds for the two larger planets (Table 2 of
https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0305536).

calibration automation remains deferred; no background run is active.

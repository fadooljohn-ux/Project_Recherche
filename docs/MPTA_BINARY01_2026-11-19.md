No candidate and no new planet. Both searched pulsars are quiet: neither peak

noise; they are detection sensitivities, not posterior limits. Each `run01`

## Why four were held

A target is searched only if the timing model stays linear over the range of
orbital parameters the search projects out. Held means not searched; it is
not a non-detection.

| Target | Parameter that fails | Departure from linear (TOA sigma; limit 0.1) | Cause |
|---|---|---:|---|
| J1909−3744 | SINI | 0.17 | Nearly edge-on orbit; Shapiro delay is curved |
| J1614−2230 | SINI | 3.0 | Same, more strongly |
| J2241−5236 | FB2 | 0.50 | Second orbital-frequency derivative (orbital variability) |
| J1811−2405 | PB | very large | Period and periastron advance are both free on a nearly circular orbit and cannot be separated |

J1909−3744 and J1614−2230 were each searched once in NANOGrav data in
September (`NO_TRIGGER`), so they are not unsearched pulsars. Searching the
held four in MeerKAT data needs a nonlinear treatment of the inner orbit, not
a looser check.

## Method

`tools/mpta_binary.py` (`tools/recherche mpta-binary
select|prepare|calibrate|freeze|run`) joins the two existing procedures:

- Noise and data handling are those of the MeerKAT isolated-pulsar batches
  ([MPTA_BATCH01](MPTA_BATCH01_2026-09-08.md)): published EFAC, EQUAD, ECORR
  and red noise held fixed; a free dispersion term per observation.
- The orbit is handled as in the NANOGrav binary pilots
  ([BINARY_PILOT01](BINARY_PILOT01_2026-09-09.md)): every free orbital
  parameter is projected together with the timing parameters, after its
  analytic derivative is checked against finite differences (limit 10⁻³) and
  the model is checked for linearity (limit 0.1 TOA sigma).
- Search range: from the larger of 30 days and ten inner periods up to 400
  days; circular outer orbit; 4,096 fixed-noise nulls per target.
- The 1% false-alarm allowance is shared across all six listed targets,
  including the four held.

**One change from the NANOGrav procedure, made before any search.** The first
preparation of J0125−2327 was held because the fitted orbit moved 5.4 released
sigma. A preparation diagnostic showed that the released MeerKAT uncertainties
are about 3.4 times smaller than the uncertainties under the published noise
model the search uses; in those units the shift is 1.5 sigma and adding the
orbital columns changes chi-square by 7.8 for 7 parameters. The check now
uses three times the larger of the two uncertainties, for both the linearity
range and the allowed shift. This widens the range that must be linear; it
is what holds J1909−3744 and J1614−2230. The failed attempt is retained.

Diagnostic injections recovered 96 of 96 and 95 of 96.

## Limits

- Fixed published noise; any crossing would have gone to the second stage.
- Local linear treatment of the inner orbit; no three-body dynamics.
- 1.6 M☉ is an assumed inner-system mass; minimum masses scale as its 2/3 power.
- PINT's approximate TCB-to-TDB conversion of the released models.

# A noise-sensitive 63-day timing feature in PSR J1327-6222

Project Recherche technical report, 9 September 2026. Private research record.

## Abstract

The second bounded UTMOST batch identified a periodic-signal candidate in public
DR1 observations of PSR J1327-6222 (B1323-62). The original 30–400-day search
returned a statistic of 50.287 at 63.259 days, exceeding the frozen threshold
26.255. A bounded review finds that this feature does not survive extension of
the released red-noise spectrum beyond its ten Fourier modes. At the refined
period, its statistic falls from 50.837 to 0.545 with 32 modes and 0.481 with
64 modes. A noise-family diagnostic gives 511 exceedances in 512 noise-only
realizations. Independent, sparse TPA measurements do not corroborate the feature
but have inadequate sensitivity to reject it. The disposition is
**INCONCLUSIVE_NOISE_SENSITIVE**, with no planetary detection claimed.

## Data and original result

The discovery dataset contains 308 UTMOST TOAs with fixed published EFAC, EQUAD
and red-noise parameters. The peak amplitude is 140.4 microseconds. The original
null reduced chi-squared is 0.998; the independent full-design likelihood check
agrees with the search statistic. Thus a satisfactory global chi-squared did not
guarantee adequate noise description at the candidate timescale.

The original search is one of 25 frozen grids sharing a conditional 1% allowance.
Its inputs, mask, threshold and result are preserved. Follow-up statistics below
do not replace that threshold or constitute a recalibrated discovery probability.
Original source and import conventions are documented in the
[batch report](UTMOST_BATCH02_2026-09-09.md) and
[UTMOST preparation report](UTMOST_PREPARATION01_2026-09-09.md).

## Methods

The standing candidate-review policy authorized this follow-up. Before the new
calculations, the saved scope fixed a 55–70-day local grid at 0.05-day spacing,
all observing-day deletions, an early/late contrast, removal of the clock-anomaly
window, eight noise alternatives, 512 null realizations, 256 injections and seed
2026091327. The original eligible period grid and local grid are searched together
in the simulation diagnostic. No blind search of an additional pulsar was added.

The covariance family retains the published white noise and compares the
released ten-mode red spectrum with 32 and 64 modes, half/double red amplitude
at 64 modes, spectral-index changes of minus/plus one, and a 64-mode frequency
scaling proportional to frequency to the power minus four. The latter is a
single-band degeneracy check, not a measurement of scattering. The published
ten-mode cutoff does not reach the candidate's approximately 63-day timescale;
the extended spectra permit stochastic power there.

The Gaussian objective includes chi-squared, log determinant of covariance and
the determinant term from marginalizing the same scaled linear timing design.
Noise models are selected separately under the null and sinusoidal hypotheses.
Every simulation repeats that model selection and the same period search.
Nulls are generated from the selected null covariance. Injections add the
refined waveform estimated from the discovery observations and therefore measure
conditional recovery, not independent validation.

The fixed-period comparison uses the existing TPA adapter on 96 public TOAs in
12 epochs spanning 477.7 days, with released dispersion, instrument and noise
terms. This dataset was excluded from the original survey for insufficient
coverage. Its exclusion remains intact. The review performs only a fixed-period
fit and analytic power calculation, not a new TPA period search.

## Results

### Sampling, observing days and time consistency

The refined peak is at 63.511985 days, with statistic 50.837 and amplitude
140.401 microseconds under the released covariance. Deleting any one observing
day leaves a statistic of at least 40.367. Removing the single observation in
the clock-anomaly window increases the statistic to 58.912. These checks argue
against a single-observation or that specific clock-window explanation.

The early half has statistic 3.768 and amplitude 64.9 microseconds; the later
half has statistic 44.254 and amplitude 176.5 microseconds. A joint common-signal
versus split-coefficient contrast gives extra chi-squared 6.985 for two degrees
of freedom, with conditional fixed-period tail 0.0304. This is descriptive
evidence of time dependence in the discovery dataset, not a standalone global
rejection test. A nearby annual sixth harmonic was also evaluated; the complete
fit and joint-template results are retained in `step1.json`.

### Noise dependence

| Covariance | Statistic at 63.512 days |
|---|---:|
| Released 10 modes | 50.837 |
| Published spectrum extended to 32 modes | 0.545 |
| Published spectrum extended to 64 modes | 0.481 |
| 64 modes, half amplitude | 3.762 |
| 64 modes, double amplitude | 0.077 |
| 64 modes, flatter spectral index | 0.055 |
| 64 modes, steeper spectral index | 5.030 |
| 64 modes with frequency-dependent scaling | 0.516 |

The preferred null is the 32-mode model. Its timing-marginalized objective is
about 102 units lower than that of the released ten-mode null. After selecting
models under both hypotheses, the family statistic is only 4.570. Of 512
noise-only realizations, 511 reach or exceed this value; the plus-one diagnostic
tail is 0.998. This is conditional on a finite fitted noise family, not a
probability that the pulsar has no planet.

Of 256 injected realizations, 135 recover a period within two days of the injected
63.512-day waveform. All exceed the weak observed family statistic. This shows
that detection yield and period recovery are different quantities; the latter
is only about 53% in this diagnostic noise model.

### Independent TPA comparison

The fixed-period TPA fit has statistic 0.827, tail probability 0.661, and fitted
amplitude 86.7 microseconds. The conditional power to detect the UTMOST waveform
at a fixed-period 1% threshold is only 11.7%. The sparse TPA sample therefore
neither corroborates nor meaningfully rules out the UTMOST waveform. No claim
of independent confirmation or exclusion is supported.

## Interpretation and limitations

The candidate is robust to individual observing-day deletion but highly sensitive
to a physically relevant noise-model extension. The combination of a better
extended-noise fit, loss of the candidate statistic and time-dependent strength
does not support a planetary interpretation from these observations.

Independent published work reports rapid variations in J1327-6222's radio-profile
shape and spin-down rate, and notes that approximately monthly observing cadence
may not resolve its underlying behavior. This supplies a relevant intrinsic
variability alternative; it does not identify our particular 63-day feature.
See [Lower et al., *The ubiquity of variable radio emission and spin-down rates
in pulsars*](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/538/4/3104/8082131), section
“PSR J1327–6222 (B1323–62)”.

UTMOST covers effectively one frequency band, so achromatic timing, dispersion,
scattering and pulse-profile changes cannot be separated confidently here. The
noise family is finite, stationary and conditional on selected parameters; the
study does not fit a full nonstationary emission-state model. Those limits do
not justify an unbounded model search or a planet claim.

The original threshold crossing remains recorded as a historical candidate.
The follow-up disposition is **INCONCLUSIVE_NOISE_SENSITIVE**. No further search,
planet fit, qualification campaign or monitoring job follows automatically.
Revisit only with materially better independent observations or a specifically
directed scientific question. Recherche remains private.

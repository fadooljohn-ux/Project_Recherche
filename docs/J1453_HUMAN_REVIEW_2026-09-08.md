# J1453+1902 four-step review — 8 September 2026

**Complete. Disposition: INCONCLUSIVE / NOISE-SENSITIVE.** The original NANOGrav
15-year wideband trigger remains a preserved search result. This review does not
support a confirmed planet or a publication-ready planet claim. It also does not
establish that no planet exists.

The owner authorized the proposed four-step “Human” Review. Codex executed the
calculations and inspected the diagnostic plots; this is agent-assisted review,
not an independent astronomer's sign-off. No public release or external message
was made.

## 1. Saved signal, aliases and observing-day influence

The diagnostic GLS fit reproduces the original statistic to numerical precision:
34.914232612986 versus the saved 34.914232612986 at 118.4272569 days. The original
threshold remains 26.8264807. No new diagnostic or subset inherits that threshold.

A predeclared 100–140-day refinement gives **118.8668263 days**, amplitude
**1.519994 microseconds**, and delta chi-square **35.319055**. Conditional on the
released fixed-noise circular model, the local profile-likelihood envelopes are
118.20–119.70 days (68%) and 117.60–120.95 days (95%), at 0.05-day grid resolution.
These are local parameter intervals after selection, not discovery confidence.

The 121.75-day annual third harmonic gives delta chi-square 29.051484. Adding
the candidate after that harmonic improves the fit by 10.035830; adding the
harmonic after the candidate gives only 3.768260. The candidate is preferred in
this comparison, but annual/sampling ambiguity is not categorically excluded.
The saved unweighted observing-window plot is a descriptive alias diagnostic.

All 73 integer TDB observing days were removed one at a time, retaining the
correct paired TOA/DM rows and refitting surviving nuisance columns. The
fixed-period statistic ranges from 19.272013 to 39.300917. Removing MJD 57389
(three TOAs) produces the largest loss. The locally refined peak remains between
118.05 and 119.25 days. The period is reasonably stable, while its strength is
sensitive to individual observations. Integer days are an operational grouping,
not a claim of statistical independence.

## 2. Time and radio-band consistency

The split uses the median unique observing day. Each subset refits its timing,
dispersion and instrumental nuisance terms with the subset covariance.

| Subset | TOAs | Fixed-period amplitude (microseconds) | Delta chi-square |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Early | 65 | 1.950 | 25.424 |
| Late | 57 | 0.527 | 1.656 |
| 430 MHz | 49 | 37.627 | 1.205 |
| L band | 73 | 3.211 | 3.860 |

The large 430-MHz fitted amplitude is poorly constrained: its sine/cosine
coefficient errors are about 39 microseconds. It is not evidence for a larger
planet. Free per-subset dispersion terms severely reduce single-band sensitivity.

Joint fits that compare a common sinusoid with separate sine/cosine coefficients
for each group give two-degree-of-freedom consistency p-values of **0.198**
(time split) and **0.103** (radio bands). These do not establish strong
inconsistency, but the later data and the band-only fits do not independently
reinforce the candidate. Every subset participated in discovery.

## 3. Noise alternatives and bounded simulations

Eight covariance choices were declared before this review's residual inspection:
released covariance; additional achromatic exponential covariance with 365-day
correlation time and RMS 0.5, 1, 2 or 4 microseconds; doubled auxiliary DM errors;
and additional frequency-dependent delays proportional to frequency^-4 with
90-day correlation time and RMS 1 or 2 microseconds at 1400 MHz.

Linear timing coefficients are marginalized with the same flat-prior measure.
The objective is chi-square + log determinant of covariance + log determinant
of the timing normal matrix, with a fixed timing-column normalization. The
sinusoid and discrete covariance choice are profiled. This is not a Bayesian
planet/no-planet evidence calculation.

The preferred null uses the 2-microsecond achromatic correlated component; the
preferred sinusoid model uses 1 microsecond. Their improvement statistic is
**16.499974**. The null correlated-noise model improves the timing-marginalized
objective by 21.963 relative to the released noise model. The candidate-period
statistic under that fixed 2-microsecond covariance is only 11.509.

One bounded exercise used seed 2026092201, actual sampling, **512 noise-only**
realizations and **256 injections**. Noise was generated from the selected null
covariance. Each realization reselected covariance separately under null and
signal and searched the union of the original eligible grid and the local
refinement grid. Timing parameters were refitted through GLS projection.

**46/512 noise-only searches** were at least as strong as observed (plus-one
estimate **0.091618**). This is a conditional single-target diagnostic, not the
original 16-target campaign false-alarm probability. Its resolution and limited
noise family are insufficient to establish a small discovery tail.

Injected signals used the refined phase and 1.520-microsecond amplitude:
197/256 recovered the period within two days, and 188/256 exceeded the observed
statistic. These sensitivity simulations are not observed planet recoveries.
The result is sufficient to classify this candidate as noise-sensitive; it does
not prove which noise process caused the original trigger.

## 4. Narrowband reduction and additional data

The official release's primary narrowband PAR/TIM files were extracted with
member names and SHA-256 hashes recorded. The fit uses all **2,551 channelized
TOAs over 71 observing days**, the released timing design, and full published
EFAC/EQUAD/ECORR covariance. No fitted noise waveform was subtracted beforehand.

At the wideband period, delta chi-square is **5.220176** and the fitted amplitude
is **1.100421 microseconds**. The strongest peak within 100–140 days is **129.35
days**, statistic **8.331190**. This alternate reduction does not strongly
reproduce the wideband trigger. The observations share the same 2013–2020 date
span; they are not independent confirmation. No narrowband search threshold was
calibrated, and this is not a new survey NO_TRIGGER result.

The bounded public-data check acquired no additional calibrated J1453 timing
series. The [NANOGrav catalog](https://nanograv.org/science/data) lists the 15-year
release as its latest primary timing dataset. The
[CPTA/FAST catalog](https://nadc.china-vo.org/res/r103117/?lang=en) describes
2019–2022 observations, but its public data access and data type identify
polarization profiles. The [CPTA noise supplement](https://zenodo.org/records/13828113)
metadata lists a single `plots_spna.zip`; its contents were not inspected, and no
directly exposed PAR/TIM files were verified. The
[noise paper](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/forth/aa52550-24.pdf) points to
that supplement. This is an acquisition limit of this review, not a claim that
no other timing data exist anywhere.

## Closeout and maintenance

Park J1453 as inconclusive pending additional calibrated timing data. The next
useful investigation would obtain FAST or another observatory's TOAs, uncertainty
and noise information, check epoch overlap, and freeze a prediction before using
new measurements. Do not retune the original threshold or keep repeating the same
dataset to obtain a favorable outcome. The broader source queue can continue as
a separate campaign.

All **231 prior search/refinement result files** were checked against their saved
hashes. Qualified `src/` code, calibration implementation, original observed
inputs and the candidate result are preserved. Four step records and the review
disposition are appended to the master workbook Work log; the target's next step
now says inconclusive/noise-sensitive. Its original candidate status remains a
historical statistical trigger, and confirmed new discoveries remain zero.
